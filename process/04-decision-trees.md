# 04 · Decision Trees (the spine of the process presentation)

The presentation principle: **every major choice becomes one fork, with the rejected branch left visible and annotated *why not*.** This directly serves what the brief rewards — "how you make decisions" and "what you deliberately chose not to build." Visually: the chosen path lit, rejected branches dimmed with a one-line reason.

Legend: ✓ chosen · ✗ rejected (with why) · ~ viable alternative

## Tree 1 — Who & when (the scenario)
```
ROOT ▸ "Put trustworthy AI into a high-stakes government moment"
│
├─ MODE ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
│   ~ Public appearance    top craft ceiling; reframed by user as a
│   │                       broad meeting-prep tool for ALL participants  → CHOSEN as A
│   ✗ Meeting (bilateral)   high impact, but broad & hard to simulate
│   ~ Decision             recurring, on-mandate (Claude's pick: procurement)
│
└─ RESULT ▸ Scenario A — a meeting-prep / briefing assistant
            (hero persona TBD; see Tree 3)
```
*Note: Claude's likelihood-weighted recommendation was the procurement "sign-off copilot" (Decision branch). The user selected the briefing-assistant (A) on the argument that it generalizes to every meeting participant. Both branches are preserved here — the rejected one is part of the story.*

## The full spine (Trees 2–6, to be built out)
2. **Which capabilities** (1–2 of 4) — chosen vs. sketched vs. cut, and why the hero isn't the briefing-assembly itself.
3. **Persona & platform / form factor** — senior-official hero vs. any participant; tablet vs. desktop; *because* of the moment of use.
